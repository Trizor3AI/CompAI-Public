# Security and storage

## Helper message bounds (0.53.16)

The plugin reads bridge lines through a fixed 4,096-character buffer and rejects more than 131,072 UTF-16 characters before materializing a complete line or parsing JSON. LF, CRLF, bare CR and a final unterminated EOF line retain their previous meaning. Cancellation and the existing owned-process cleanup remain in effect.

The Codex adapter uses a buffered binary pipe with a bounded read of at most 8 MiB plus one byte. A single synchronized inbox retains raw frames, with a shared 32 MiB and 1,024-message budget covering both new and postponed RPC messages. Only one frame is decoded at a time; postponed entries retain raw bytes and a scalar correlation ID. Overflow or malformed input sets a sanitized terminal error, releases queued frames and wakes waiting consumers without blocking the producer or silently dropping events. Known failed or ended transports reject new sends. A write already in flight remains subject to the existing outer request watchdog and process cleanup. Reader cancellation does not prevent the short acknowledgement/interrupt cleanup sequence.

The frame limit accommodates the permitted aggregate image attachment size after base64 encoding; it is a conservative contract-derived limit, not a measured runtime peak. Final answer envelopes are bounded at 72,256 characters before trimming or JSON parsing, including escaped Unicode; decoded answers retain their existing 6,000-character limit. These limits bound transport payloads, not total Python/native-provider memory or provider token usage. Permissions, tool budgets, account behavior and automatic retry policy are unchanged. Synthetic transport tests do not establish live-provider behavior or an operating-system memory-isolation boundary.

CompAI is a read-only game companion. Settings control access to inventory, retainers, saddlebag, housing, FC chest, currencies, duty observations, quests, character/equipment, location, NPC dialogue/target, Grand Company deliveries, older chat lookup, and online information. Allowed local tools retrieve relevant records when requested; they do not attach every saved record to every question. Selected Field Record and Portable Archive observations use the existing Collection Tools permission; Mahjong has a separate default-off schema23 grant. Wire37 is required for new book categories and available Mahjong fields. Bounded readers corroborate exact owned visible menus, repeated stable text and character identity; no menu opening or server refresh occurs. Archive checks supported local title/body definitions but emits only title and page references, not body text. Mahjong preserves six displayed labels rather than deriving rank progress. These observations expire after 15 minutes in the current login and are cleared on owner/session or permission changes. English-only book layouts fail closed for unsupported display languages.


Cloud requests contain your question, selected conversation context, personality instructions, and results of permitted tools. Public game-data lookups contact fixed XIVAPI, Universalis or xivgear endpoints. Web search may use queries derived from the question and conversation. An allowed search query is still data sent outside your PC.

Public xivgear retrieval uses the existing online-information permission and only share UUIDs parsed from literal supported HTTPS links in the current question or supplied user-question history. Assistant text and model-supplied URLs cannot grant a share. The bridge checks that binding before cache access, then issues fixed GET requests to `api.xivgear.app/shortlink/<UUID>` and optionally `/fulldata` with a canonical share URL and bounded set index. Arbitrary destinations, redirects, credentials and private gear uploads are excluded. HTTP responses cap at 256 KiB; normalized results at 20,000 bytes, with up to 24 set summaries and one selected set. The existing bounded public cache can retain these results for one day; share IDs and plan names are local query history, not anonymous data. Custom items/foods and special-stat plans are rejected. Descriptions, simulations and arbitrary nested fields are omitted; returned names remain untrusted. Computed totals preserve reference-plan settings, including xivgear's HQ food/medicine assumptions, and do not establish current equipment, consumption, ownership, DPS or optimal melds.

Saved-item batch pricing requires both online information and the selected storage permissions. A frozen candidate list stays on the local plugin/bridge pipe; Universalis receives item IDs and the selected world/data center, not character names, retainer names, slots, quantities or chats. Only the ranked shortlist, its saved locations and coverage summary reach the AI provider. The private pipe has a separate two-response allowance (512 KiB candidates, 128 KiB winner details); ordinary reader limits are unchanged and the final pricing result is limited to 20,000 bytes. Each question may run one valuation over at most 4,096 item/quality groups, with public requests in batches of at most 100 IDs, bounded request counts, cancellation and a total time budget. Missing quotes and interrupted batches remain explicit. Existing retainer offers and placed furnishings are excluded; FC/housing storage requires an explicit selected category.

Valuation ranks the lower of observed asking and median recent sale prices, or the median alone when no asking price is reported. At least three matching-quality transactions are required from a sample of at most 20 recent sales across both qualities within seven days. Thin samples are unknown, not zero value. No buyer/seller identities are retained. New scans may reuse per-item/world quotes for 60 seconds and refresh only on demand; original fetch and upstream upload times remain attached. A scan continuation may retain older observations as described below. An upload over one hour old or with an unknown time receives an advisory outdated notice. A recent upload does not establish freshness across every world, sale eligibility or guaranteed proceeds.

Corrupt or unavailable cache storage does not replace a successful public response. The embedded Python runtime supplies SQLite; no database service or additional download is needed.

An OS lock and small timestamp-only state under the public cache coordinate conservative request pacing and HTTP 429 cooldowns; they contain no holdings, account identifiers or credentials. Requests recheck permission, cancellation and deadlines while waiting. Matching normalized cache entries are rechecked after admission without resetting their age. This is not a strict FIFO queue or a guarantee against provider rate limits. Waiting does not extend the pricing/question budgets, and no AI question is retried automatically. Invalid coordination state fails closed for market requests.

Pricing continuation stores at most eight checkpoints with hashes, timestamps, progress and an opaque reference to a local quote ledger bounded at 6 MiB. The ledger retains public quote observations and original timestamps for that scan, not private quantities, names, slots or credentials. Nevertheless, its item IDs can reveal which items were queried: treat both the cache and scan ledger as private local history, excluded from publication and packages. Checkpoints and ledgers become unusable ten minutes after the scan starts; cleanup occurs during subsequent scan activity, not in a background deletion task. A fresh private snapshot must match the plugin session, character, access epoch, selected sources and contents; world, ranking and category selection must also match. Timestamp-only observation refreshes do not change that binding. Only the current explicit `Continue pricing` or `Resume pricing` message enables continuation, including if the model omits its resume flag. A model-requested resume without that user intent is denied before private callbacks or HTTP. Reloading the plugin invalidates its session binding. Existing one-valuation-per-question and time limits remain. Continuing retains already gathered observations even after the ordinary 60-second freshness window; a new scan must refresh those expired quotes. Retained observations are never relabelled as a simultaneous live market snapshot.

Tool discovery exposes only permitted capabilities. The shared application dispatcher requires discovery before invocation and then applies the original argument/result validators, per-reader permissions, cancellation, identity checks and attribution. Discovery permits four calls, up to three names per call, and 48,000 bytes of application metadata; execution keeps the separate 12-call/120,000-byte shared reader limits. Correlation IDs are unique across both paths. Discovery metadata is application-authored usage guidance, not retrieved game text. Reader results remain untrusted data, except validated persona details retain their existing limited style authority. No native filesystem, shell or unrestricted HTTP capability is added. Grok's native turn ceiling is 19 to accommodate bounded discovery; application limits still govern execution.

## Local storage

The four storage readers are separate, default-off permissions. They copy bounded loaded containers while the matching game menu is open, checking character and source identity before and after capture. Retainer listings mean current asking prices, not completed sales. Housing distinguishes property/room and placed versus stored furnishings. FC chest attribution requires a matching FC/member list containing the local character. Unavailable containers preserve historical observations rather than reporting zero items. No network interception, menu automation or item movement is used.

Storage observations use schema 2 of the per-character cache; schema 1 inventory/quest files remain readable. Exact internal retainer, property and FC identity keys stay local. Permitted tool results include human-readable source labels, which may disclose retainer names or property locations, plus item data and timestamps. A frozen query is bound to its character and cache-load epoch; access is rechecked for each callback. Disabling a reader stops new capture and tool access but retains its cache until cleared. Recovery files can retain earlier observations.

Unified saved-item search delegates to those same carried-bag and storage callbacks. It adds no persistent index or identity and does not bypass per-reader permissions, validation or shared call/result budgets. Each selected underlying reader consumes a call; results retain separate source coverage. Scoped clear confirmations bind to the character/cache epoch, cancel pending questions and preserve unrelated sources. Clearing active observations does not remove recovery copies or prevent a still-open game menu from being captured again.

New installations store configuration under `%APPDATA%/XIVLauncher/pluginConfigs/CompAI/`. This includes saved chats, presets, access preferences, character caches, and exports. The companion keeps a separate provider profile; treat that entire profile as private, including credentials, databases, and logs.

CompAI's ChatGPT/Codex sign-in uses locally cached login tokens, not a separate API key. The pinned runtime is explicitly configured with `cli_auth_credentials_store="keyring"`: it uses the Windows credential store and fails if that store is unavailable. CompAI does not select `file` or `auto`, which could allow a plaintext `auth.json`. The dedicated profile path scopes the login; credentials are not stored beside the plugin binaries. See [OpenAI's credential-storage documentation](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/auth#credential-storage). Windows-protected credentials can still be accessed by malicious software running as the same Windows user; hiding a file would not provide equivalent protection.

The Storage page measures known local categories only after an explicit refresh. Its worker reads bounded filesystem metadata, rejects detected linked paths and never scans the provider profile. Paths are displayed locally, not attached to questions or the copyable diagnostics report. Counts are point-in-time estimates, not a consistency snapshot. No deletion is performed.

Character-cache updates retain one previous valid file (`.bak`) and, when replacing a fully readable but corrupt file, one quarantined copy (`.quarantine`). These files are for manual recovery and are never restored automatically or queried by the AI. Clear removes the selected category from the active cache; recovery files may still contain older values. An unsupported schema or a file that cannot be safely inspected is protected from replacement.

Chat exports and recovery copies are kept under `exports/chats/` in the configuration directory. They include the full conversation, draft, notes and attached personality, and can contain private game context. They are separate from the active chat files: deleting a chat or its 30-day inactivity expiry does not delete exports. Remove exports yourself when no longer needed. Failed writes remain in memory while the plugin is loaded; a warning does not guarantee a recovery file was successfully saved.

Chat names, pins and ordering are stored separately in `chats/sidebar-order.json`. Pinned chats are exempt from automatic inactivity expiry; unreadable or unsupported sidebar metadata suspends expiry to preserve unknown pins. Bulk deletion targets only explicitly selected chat IDs and refuses chats with pending answers or unsaved changes. Exports retain their original automatic title rather than the sidebar name. Local search does not send query text or results to the AI.

Up to two question processes can run at once against the private provider profile. Each request owns its process, cancellation, reader callbacks and destination chat. It receives only that chat's frozen context. Account login/logout and synthetic tests are exclusive. Currency and duty captures are held only for their requesting answer, and model-supplied IDs never become arbitrary native memory calls.

Attachment drafts and retry bytes are held in bounded memory, associated with their original chat, and cleared on deletion/disposal or successful use. They are not automatically added to subsequent questions. Saved chat files retain filenames/types/sizes rather than the uploaded bytes; exports and model answers may nevertheless quote private attachment contents. Provider handling is separate from the plugin's local retention, and the private provider profile should still be treated as sensitive.

Upgrades from `Inspector` preserve recognized saved data and retain the original companion profile path so the existing scoped login continues to work. A fixed-content completion receipt in the legacy directory makes the import one-time, including for previously migrated installations. Resetting CompAI afterward starts with fresh settings and a new CompAI account profile instead of restoring the old permissions, chats or spoiler preference. Malformed or linked receipts and failed receipt writes stop migration rather than reimporting data. Do not share either configuration directory in bug reports. Saved data is ordinary local storage, not an encrypted vault.

## Runtime boundary

Diagnostics retains bounded numeric measurements and fixed activity labels in memory, separating at most two active requests and the latest completion from app-session samples. Request timings, reported tokens and observed tool/web-search/reasoning-item counts belong to their operation; replaced or completed operations cannot update another request. Event counts stop at 1,024 unique events per category. Tool counts include discovery; reasoning items are not model API calls, and the API-call count is unavailable. Diagnostics records no prompt/answer text, tool arguments, paths, character/account IDs or authentication data. Copying the report is explicit; measurements are cleared on plugin reload and are not uploaded by the plugin. The chat status/hover reads this metadata without filesystem or network work.

Activity reply compaction is a provider-facing projection after full transport validation, frozen identity/row checks and original-byte accounting. Explicit null defaults apply only to omitted entry fields; missing rows and result metadata retain their existing meanings. It does not expand permissions, waive a result budget, persist observations, or turn unknown values into zero. Immutable snapshot promotion preserves the observation timestamp and still requires the existing settling, session, ownership and expiry checks.

Shop highlighting is a separate default-off Beta choice and also requires Shops access. Only offers actually returned by a permitted lookup can create a session-only action. The complete saved offer list must match the currently open ordinary gil shop; the action then binds to that current native shop identity, not a claim about the historical vendor. Addresses and action keys never enter model payloads or saved chats. Character/session/area/access changes and shop lifecycle changes revoke actions; item, quality, bundle and price are rechecked before highlighting. Candidate handles are bounded and expire within five minutes; the manual overlay lasts at most fifteen seconds. Unsupported, clipped or unverified rows are not outlined. It never opens or scrolls a shop, selects a purchase, or submits a transaction. The historical shop answer remains usable if optional highlighting cannot be established.

The private runtime is started suspended and assigned to an unnamed Windows job before execution. Closing the owning bridge also closes the job and terminates its runtime descendants, including children whose parent has already exited. No unrelated process is targeted. Failure to establish containment stops startup.

The bridge resolves a private runtime bundle and checks the exact hashes of both executables and its validated tool configuration before launch. Public game-data requests use fixed hosts, bounded results, and reject redirects. The runtime tool restrictions have loopback positive/negative checks; executable pins are integrity checks, not a substitute for trusting the distributor or reviewing the runtime.

The bridge launches only the pinned embedded Python beside the plugin. Its complete flat file set and SHA-256 hashes are checked before launch, and an isolated startup probe checks the interpreter version and required standard-library imports. Unexpected payload files, missing dependencies or tampering stop startup. The isolated search path excludes system/user Python packages and environment overrides; it explicitly includes the trusted adjacent bridge modules. There is no `PATH` fallback or runtime download during plugin use. As with the Codex pin, this protects package integrity rather than establishing trust in a maliciously modified distributor or host.

## Additional account runtimes

Grok is an experimental Free-account provider; Copilot remains disabled at both UI and bridge entry points pending authenticated free-account validation. The account store encrypts credentials with Windows CurrentUser DPAPI, uses provider-specific files and cross-process refresh locks, and writes only ciphertext to disk. It does not make credentials inaccessible to malware running as the same Windows user. Corrupt or foreign-user vaults fail closed and are not silently replaced.

Copilot's source implementation uses CompAI's registered public OAuth client, device flow and expiring tokens. Only the public Client ID is shipped in source; no client secret is used. Token callbacks are bound to the checked account and session, and ambient CLI sign-in is disabled. Clearing saved sign-in removes only CompAI's local encrypted credential; it does not revoke authorization on GitHub. Encrypted provider profiles are still excluded from publication and ordinary project backups.

Grok device sign-in uses fixed official HTTPS authorization/token endpoints and the official public client contract, with redirects and inherited proxies disabled, bounded responses and polling, cancellation and expiry. CompAI opens the validated device URL through the normal system browser, like Codex. Only the access token is retained; refresh tokens are discarded and expiry requires sign-in again. Before replacing the encrypted vault, a temporary DPAPI-protected token is passed through the runtime's fixed session-auth helper using process environment only. The fixed helper is checked locally before requesting native authentication. The runtime must return the same token and accept cached authentication before the saved account can change. A locked comparison prevents an overlapping logout or account replacement from being overwritten. Cancelled or failed sign-in preserves the earlier vault. No token or protected handoff is added to command arguments, persisted configuration or diagnostic output.

Normal sign-in therefore avoids the runtime's private browser launch. The pinned runtime can still fall back to its own interactive login if its external helper fails after the local check; `headless` does not disable this upstream behavior. That exceptional path can open another browser window, but the native persistence guard remains active and a different returned token cannot replace the pending sign-in. This limitation also affects saved-session import. Local helper checks reduce predictable failures; they do not prove the absence of every native or operating-system failure.

A uniquely held empty file continues to block native credential persistence for each runtime lifetime. The helper is bound to the expected token, preventing a mid-session account change; unsupported helper paths fail closed. Native credential behavior remains tied to the pinned executable. Login URL and browser-choice validation stay in the plugin, and the manual URL/code remains available. The new device/browser flow has focused synthetic tests; live browser/sign-in behavior must be checked separately after installing this build.

Grok's **Clear saved sign-in** is an explicit local-only recovery action. The bridge removes only the selected installation's `providers/grok/credentials/grok.dpapi` file through the existing locked credential store, without decrypting it, checking or launching Grok, or contacting a server. The plugin accepts its exact local-completion result only for a Grok logout operation and keeps requests blocked until a later successful sign-in/status check. This does not claim provider-side revocation, erase backups or clear another installation's credentials. The bundled Python bridge is still required.

Grok checks Free billing state before opening the session for each question and rejects positive paid allowances or automatic top-ups. Incoming messages share a limit of 1,024 messages and 32 MiB with replies held for later processing. Exceeding either bound ends the request. API-key authentication is disabled. Authenticated registry checks require only CompAI's explicit tools, with inherited managed connectors disabled. Curated native tools expose only the wrapper for exact qualified CompAI tool names; unknown permission requests, filesystem reads and shell operations are denied. This is not a full operating-system sandbox: the pinned native executable remains trusted. The online-information setting still gates CompAI's public game-data tools.

Native loopback denials and encrypted resume passed. One corrected synthetic Free-account tool roundtrip also passed after an earlier question failed closed on tool permissions; neither used game data or saved chats. No Copilot model request was sent. Grok can make additional title requests and internal retries, so one application question is not necessarily one provider inference call. The adapter does not automatically retry questions or fall back to paid credits. Auto reasoning was live-tested; explicit reasoning requires native confirmation before a prompt. Hosted web search and images remain unsupported on Grok.

The prototype free-only checks reject paid or unverified billing routes rather than enabling top-ups or using API credentials. Missing usage counters remain unknown. Before activation, secure credential storage, authenticated tool boundaries, cancellation and actual free-account responses must be verified together. A pin or passing mock alone is insufficient.

## Repository and reports

The Release package verifier opens the compiled plugin and reads its embedded debug information. It checks that every source file uses a neutral path, that the symbols omit source text and Source Link records, and that the assembly carries the release version without private repository metadata.

Credentials, real captures, exports, saved chats, generated binaries, and internal working notes are excluded from the source repository. Synthetic fixtures and runtime validation reports are kept for reproducible checks. Do not add personal data to a fixture.

The offline publication guard checks source paths and recognizable credential content before publication; the release verifier also checks textual package assets. Neither prints matched secrets. Encrypted credential vaults are excluded too: encryption does not make a profile suitable for publication. These checks and the explicit package allowlist reduce accidental disclosure risk but do not prove that arbitrary content is free of private data.

With checkout-local `core.hooksPath=.githooks`, normal Git pushes must pass the pre-push guard. It inspects outgoing committed file versions and commit/tag messages, including a secret deleted in a later commit. New refs or unavailable remote bases conservatively scan full reachable history. Missing Python, unreadable history or a detected issue stops the push. This is not server enforcement: a deliberately bypassed hook or connector/API upload can evade it. Connector publication must run `publication_guard.py --outgoing <local-object-id> --base <remote-object-id>` before uploading the corresponding committed bytes; see BUILDING.md. Source checks do not replace the binary package allowlist or scanning for unknown secret formats.

Report a suspected issue privately to the repository owner before sharing credentials or a reproduction containing personal data. Include the plugin version, a short description, and a synthetic example where possible. A source review and passing tests do not establish that software has no vulnerabilities.

Native reads stay on the framework thread under character, owner, cache-epoch, zone, permission and cancellation guards. Public catalog caches and answer sessions recheck sheet identity, game-data identity and language.

Fishing and spearfishing read only named ordinary Item references from `FishParameter.IsInLog` and `SpearfishingItem.IsVisible` entries respectively. Their IDs retain those sheet namespaces. The [pinned managed helpers](https://github.com/aers/FFXIVClientStructs/blob/d6397a76b3a63c2b247025dc296653182b718ca6/FFXIVClientStructs/FFXIV/Client/Game/UI/PlayerState.cs) define the caught-flag mapping; the reader checks both helper bounds and physical buffer capacity before invoking them. Unsupported entries stay unknown, and a native false never establishes that a fish was not caught. Bounded framework-thread snapshots recheck owner, buffer stability, session/zone, cancellation and public catalog identity, then remain frozen only for their answer. These observations are not persisted as personal log caches and do not establish inventory, catch size, records or fishing conditions. Saved answers may still contain previously returned observations.

Reader instructions retain shared privacy, evidence and result-budget rules once per tool family, and include category-specific guidance only for granted categories. Public tool discovery includes item, market and gear-plan guidance only for the tools being discovered. Omission of irrelevant guidance never changes validation, permission checks or data access.

## Saved progression, map links and clipboard images

Version 0.34.0 samples only permitted, visible Shared FATE and Trust menus every 500 ms. Two stable observations are required; closed, invalid or changing menus cannot replace saved pages. Character-cache schema 3 retains bounded managed page copies with their original observation times (at most 3 FATE tabs and 80 Trust groups of 12 members). Old schemas 1–2 load with no invented progression. Unchanged page timestamps are persisted at most once per minute. Results freeze for an answer, carry per-row observation time/page, and remain last-known data with incomplete coverage. Owner/session/access epochs and cancellation are rechecked before return; offline use performs no native reads. Revocation stops capture and use without deleting data. Identity-bound Clear cancels questions, deletes the selected category and disables it to prevent immediate refill.

Wire v29 adds public map definitions to `query_game_definitions`, using `local_game_definitions_v3`; older definition scopes keep their previous shapes. Map/territory IDs and optional floor names come from installed sheets. A `compai-map://territory/map?x=...&y=...` destination has a strict bounded grammar and display coordinates from 1 through 100; IDs must match current local sheets before a payload is created. These links are local actions, never web sources or shell/browser destinations. Plugin clicks marshal native UI work to the framework thread and recheck login/session/disposal. Coordinates may still be wrong or ambiguous despite a valid map/territory pair. [Dalamud's map-link implementation](https://github.com/goatcorp/Dalamud/blob/4ec141d51f98275df1f22ea92c1b7ab46c1ccdda/Dalamud/Game/Text/SeStringHandling/Payloads/MapLinkPayload.cs) supplies coordinate conversion and the game payload.

Version 0.35.0 places validated native map spans inline in the original Debug prose, with at most eight unique resolution attempts and eight clickable spans per answer. Verified labels replace model labels for active links; unresolved, oversized or excess references remain plain text. Application-owned map/link/color payloads stay separate from sanitized model text, and user-input echoes never create actions. A location label and its native terminator are kept together when messages wrap; surrounding prose retains the configured color. Each final encoded line is limited to 800 bytes, with a bounded plain-text fallback or rejection if encoding exceeds that limit. No standalone Map line is appended and rendering never opens the map. Offline encoding fixtures do not establish in-game clicking or color behavior.

Clipboard images are read only after Ctrl+V in the focused composer. A clipboard sequence check rejects a changed clipboard during import. PNG and supported 24/32-bit DIB formats are validated and bounded before allocation/conversion; unsupported masks/profiles are rejected. Conversion runs on a worker and retains original-chat ownership. The resulting PNG uses the existing attachment byte/count/session limits and image-capable model gate. Clipboard handles are released, unload cancels imports, and no image is automatically sent or saved to disk. Ordinary text paste remains the input widget's responsibility.
